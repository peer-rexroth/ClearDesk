Getting started

Adding and opening tasks
Add a task: type in Add a task… at the top and press Enter. The field is left and the new task is selected in the list, so Tab adds a subtask to it right away; press ↑ or N to go back and add another task. Shorthand sets fields as you type — !high !med !low (!! for high), #project, @tag and a date word (today, tomorrow, a weekday, next week, in 3 days, 2026-12-24). A preview under the bar shows what it understood; Shift+Enter adds the text exactly as typed.
Where it goes: a #project you type wins; otherwise the project you are viewing; otherwise the default project picked at the right end of the bar; otherwise Inbox. In My Day an undated task gets today's date.
Click selects, double-click opens. A single click only selects a task. A double-click anywhere on it (list row or board card) opens it in the side panel; a double-click on the title text renames it instead. Cards and rows behave the same.
Rename: double-click the title or a subtask’s text, or press F2 on the selected task. Enter saves, Esc cancels.
Selecting several tasks
Cmd/Ctrl+click adds or removes a task, Shift+click selects a range, Cmd/Ctrl+A selects every visible task. The title shows how many are selected.
The toolbar above the list — Complete, Priority, Due, Move to | Duplicate, Delete — acts on the whole selection (or on the one selected task) and is greyed out when nothing is selected. The right-click menu does the same.
The row menu
Right-click a task (or use its ⋯ button, or the Menu key / Shift+F10) for Open details, Mark complete, then Priority, Status, Due and Move to as submenus, then Add subtask, Duplicate and Delete. It acts on the whole selection when you right-click a selected task, and never changes the selection.
Due offers Today, Tomorrow, Next week, Clear — and a date field for any other date. The same menu (with the same field) opens from the toolbar’s calendar button and from the Due cell.
Undo: Cmd/Ctrl+Z takes back the last edit or delete, and the toast that appears offers Undo too.
Priority, Status and Due
Click a pill to change it where it is: Priority, Status and Due on a list row; Status and Due on a board card. A small menu opens with a tick on the current value. On the selected (blue) row the pills show a chevron, like a pop-up button.
A card or row without a due date shows a dash (list) or a dashed calendar chip on hover (board) — click it to set one.
Action buttons — Duplicate, Delete and ⋯ (plus Skip for repeating tasks) — appear at the right of a task’s line when you hover it or select it, on rows and on cards.
The side panel
Double-click a task to open it in the side panel: project, status, priority, due date, tags, repeat, notes and the full subtask tree. It stays docked next to the list, so ↑ ↓ move through tasks and the panel follows. Esc or ✕ closes it.
Tick subtasks, add new ones in the Add subtask field and drag them to reorder — the panel works like the list.
Sorting
Click a column header (Task, Project, Priority, Status, Due) to sort ascending, again for descending, a third time to go back to Smart order (by priority, due date and status). Undated tasks stay last, and completed tasks sink to the bottom either way. The Sort menu in the toolbar picks a column too.
Four levels of detail
A task can hold subtasks, which hold steps, which hold sub-steps — four levels in all. Every level has its own round checkbox.
Add one: select a task and press Tab, or right-click → Add subtask, or click the + that appears beside a line with no children; in the panel use the Add subtask field. Enter adds it, Tab / Shift+Tab in the new field nests it one level deeper or back out.
The chevron left of a line collapses or opens its children. The double-chevron button above the list folds every task at once; {K("Space")} folds the selected task.
Drag a line to reorder it — the whole line is the handle. Subtasks and steps can also move between parents; sub-steps only reorder within their own step. The indent / outdent buttons that appear on hover change a line’s level.
Board cards show subtasks too, but they stay lighter: you can tick, rename and add subtasks there, while steps and sub-steps are read and tick only.
Repeating tasks
Set Repeat in the side panel: daily, weekly, monthly or yearly, every N, on chosen weekdays, with an optional end date or a maximum number of times.
Completing a repeating task moves it to its next date and resets its subtasks instead of finishing it; the Skip button on its line does the same without completing. Undo brings the old date back.
Finding your way
The sidebar lists All Tasks, My Day (due today), This Week, Overdue, then your projects and a collapsible Tags list, each with a count. Drag the sidebar's right edge to make it wider or narrower (double-click the edge to reset; with the edge focused, the arrow keys resize it too).
List shows one line per task with Project, Priority, Status and Due columns. Board shows cards in columns — switch with the List / Board buttons, and choose Project | Status to decide what the columns are. Dragging a card to another column changes that field.
Search (/) matches titles, notes, subtasks and tags. Filter narrows by status, priority and tag; a count appears on the button and ✕ clears it. Sort picks the order.
The small slider icon beside + in the Projects heading chooses which projects appear in All Tasks and as board columns.
Sidebar
The sidebar button at the top of the sidebar (it floats at the top left while the sidebar is hidden), or ⌥⌘+S (Ctrl+Alt+S), hides and shows the sidebar. In Tasks Cmd/Ctrl+B does it too; in Notes that is Bold. In a narrow window it starts hidden and floats over the list; choosing a project closes it again.
Projects
Create projects with + in the sidebar; each gets an icon. Inbox always exists and catches tasks with no project.
Deleting a project moves its open tasks to Inbox so they can be triaged; its completed tasks are kept out of the way but still show in All Tasks, My Day, This Week, Overdue and search.
The default project for quick-add is the picker at the right end of the Add a task bar. When you are viewing a project it shows that one (greyed) instead.
Tags
Tags (waiting on, deep work, someday) are added to a task as chips in the side panel. On a list row they show as pills next to the title, on a board card as coloured strips. Click one in the sidebar to filter by it.
Writing notes
Switch between Tasks and Notes with the switch beside the app name. Notes are kept in folders: the sidebar lists All Notes, Pinned (every pinned note, from any folder) and your folders, the middle column lists the notes (newest edit first) and the right side is the editor.
New note: the New button, or N. Give it a title and start typing — it saves as you type. A note you leave completely empty is removed again.
Formatting: the toolbar has a text style (Body, Title, Heading, Subheading), bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, bulleted and numbered lists, checklists (click a circle to tick it), links (Cmd/Ctrl+K) and clear formatting. Cmd/Ctrl+B, Cmd/Ctrl+I and Cmd/Ctrl+U work as usual, and Tab indents a list item. Cmd/Ctrl+click opens a link.
Links, e-mail addresses and phone numbers (also the link button, ⌘K: type a web address, an e-mail address or a phone number; right-click a link to open, copy, edit or remove it) are recognised and linked: a web or e-mail address as soon as you type the space after it, and everything on a line (phone numbers too) when you finish it with ↵ or move to another line; also when a note opens. ⌘-click opens one (in a locked note a plain click does). Web addresses open in a new tab, e-mail addresses in your mail app, phone numbers in your phone app.
Pin a note: ⋯ → Pin Note (or right-click it in the list) keeps it at the top of its list under a Pinned heading. Unpin Note puts it back.
Recently Deleted: deleting a note moves it to Recently Deleted at the bottom of the sidebar, where it stays for 30 days. Open it there to Restore it or Delete Now; Delete All empties the list. Deleting a note that is already there removes it for good.
Text colour and highlight: select some text and use the A button in the toolbar. The first swatch of each row removes the colour. Colours adapt to light and dark mode.
Markdown shortcuts: at the start of a line type # ## ### for a title, heading or subheading, [] for a checklist, > for a quote, - / 1. for lists; --- then ↵ makes a divider and ``` then ↵ a code block. Quote and Code are also in the text style menu. In a code block press ↵ twice at the end (or ⌘↵) to carry on below it, and ⌫ at its start turns it back into normal text.
Export and print: ⋯ → Export saves the note as a Markdown file or opens Print / Save as PDF (⌘P) for this note only. Copy note puts it on the clipboard for pasting into Mail.
Search: while you search, the matches are highlighted in the open note and the first one is scrolled into view.
List styles: the list button opens a menu to pick the bullet (• ▪ – —) or the numbering (1. a. i.) for the list you are in. The current style is ticked; pick it again to turn the list off.
Sorting: the ⋯ button above the note list sorts by date edited, date created or title, newest or oldest first (A to Z for titles). The choice is remembered on this device, and the date on each row follows it.
Tables: the table button opens a size grid: move over it (or use the arrow keys) to light up the rows and columns you want, then click (or press Enter) to insert it with a header row. The grid grows up to 10×10. While your cursor is in a table with the cursor in a table, a small ··· tab appears above its column and a ⋮ tab beside its row (as in Apple Notes): click one to select that column or row, drag the round handles on its outline to take in neighbouring columns or rows, then right-click inside the selection (or on its tab) for its options — add before/after, delete, header row, delete table. The options act on everything selected. The arrow keys move between cells in every direction: + adds a row below or a column to the right, − deletes the current row or column, plus the header-row toggle and delete table. To insert above or to the left, use the table button's menu. Point at a table for a + on its right and bottom edge to add a column or row. Inside a table the toolbar table button also offers the same actions, toggles the header row, or deletes the table. Tab moves to the next cell (and adds a row after the last one). Tables pasted from the web or a spreadsheet are kept, without their colours or merged cells. All of it works with Undo. Increase Width and Decrease Width (in the table menu, and the whole-table menu) change how wide the table is, in steps: a new two-column table is about 44% of the note, then 66%, 88% and the full width, and back down to 35%.
Lock: the lock button at the right of a note's toolbar (next to ⋯) locks and unlocks it, and the note list's right-click menu has Lock too: a locked note is read-only (the lock button turns amber and closes while it is locked). You cannot type, format, tick checklists, move or delete it until you unlock it. Cloning a locked note works and keeps the lock.
Clone (in the same ⋯ menu, with Move to and Delete) makes a copy named “… (copy)” in the same folder, and opens it. The toast has an Undo.
Right-click a note for Lock, Clone, Move to and Delete, or a folder for New note here, Rename and Delete.
Pasting keeps headings, bold, italic, lists and links from other apps and cleans everything else to plain text. Images and attachments are not supported yet.
Folders & finding notes
Folders: + beside Folders adds one; hover a folder to edit or delete it. Deleting a folder moves its notes to Notes. Move a note with the folder menu in its toolbar. Deleting, moving or renaming shows an Undo toast, and ⌘Z does the same while you are not typing; inside a note, ⌘Z undoes your typing.
Subfolders: right-click a folder and choose New subfolder, or pick a location when you create or edit a folder. Folders nest one level. A folder shows its own notes plus those of its subfolders, and its count includes them. Deleting a folder deletes its subfolders too and moves all their notes to Notes (with Undo).
Reordering folders: drag a folder up or down within its level, or right-click it and choose Move up / Move down. The order, renames and moves sync between devices like note edits.
Search (/) in the sidebar looks through the titles and text of every note, whatever folder you are in, and opens the first result.
Delete a note with the trash button, or Delete while the note list has focus. ↑ ↓ move through the list and Enter jumps into the note.
Notes are part of your data: they are saved in this browser, included in Export, Import and Backups, and kept in the linked file. When two copies of a note differ, the newer edit wins.
Reusable prompts
Switch to Prompts with the switch beside the app name. A prompt is a template you reuse: write it once, put variables in it, choose their values and copy the finished text. Prompts live in folders (one level of subfolders, like Notes), and the sidebar also has Favorites and Recently Deleted. N makes a new prompt.
The template is the monospace field: write the prompt and add placeholders. {{name}} is a choice (one option), [[name]] is a checklist (any number of items, copied as a bullet list) and ((name)) is text you type each time. The three buttons above the template insert one at the caret (select a word first to use it as the name). Put a backslash in front (\{{name}}) to keep a placeholder as plain text.
Variables are on the right, in a panel that slides in and out (the panel button above it, or Option+Cmd+I). Each placeholder in the template gets a row: click an option to choose it (click it again to clear it), tick checklist items, or type the text. + adds an option or an item; to remove one, click the × that appears inside it when you point at it, select it and press Delete, or right-click it and choose Remove. To rename an option, item, preset or variable, double-click it (or select it and press F2, or right-click → Rename); Return keeps the new name and Esc leaves it. Renaming a variable renames its placeholder in the template too. Presets save a combination of choices and checklists under a name (typed text is never part of a preset); Clear empties every variable.
Copy Prompt (or Cmd/Ctrl+Enter) puts the finished text on the clipboard with every placeholder filled in. A toast tells you if a choice or a text field was left empty.
Favorite adds a prompt to the Favorites list. Lock makes it read-only (its template, name and options); you can still choose values and type text. Clone, Move to a folder and Delete are in the ⋯ menu and on right-click; you can also drag a prompt onto a folder. Delete goes to Recently Deleted for 30 days.
From PromptLab: Import PromptLab Library… in the list's ⋯ menu reads a PromptLab export (.json). Its categories become folders; favorites, locks and presets come along, version history does not. Importing the same file twice changes nothing.
Look and feel
The aA button at the bottom of the sidebar opens Appearance: Text size (Medium or Large) and Theme — Light, Dark, or Auto, which follows your system and switches live.
These are per-device choices — saved in this browser, not in your data file — so each machine can follow its own system. The same goes for the sidebar state, the board grouping and the default project.
The sidebar is frosted glass over a soft backdrop; the list, board and side panel are flat and opaque. With Reduce transparency or Increase contrast on in your system settings the glass becomes solid.
Where your tasks live
Everything is stored in this browser on this device (localStorage). There is no server and no account.
Link file (Chrome or Edge) saves every change to a real .json file on your disk, so your tasks survive a cleared browser and can be shared through a synced folder. The top-bar chip shows Link file (not linked), Linked, or Reconnect when the browser needs your permission again.
If two copies changed, they are merged: the newer edit of a task wins, and something you deleted stays deleted even when an older copy elsewhere still has it.
Backups and moving data
The Data menu (drive icon, bottom of the sidebar) can export a backup file, import one — merging with or replacing what you have (a replace backs up first) — and open Backups.
Daily snapshots of the last seven days are kept automatically; pick one under Data → Backups to restore it.
Nothing leaves your machine. There is no server, no account and no admin rights are needed. Link a file to be safe against clearing browser data.
